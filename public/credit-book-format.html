The rule: one page per customer

Not one long list for everyone.

On a shared list you can see that someone took goods on Tuesday. On their own page you can see that it's the fifth time this month, they haven't paid since the 2nd, and how much they owe now.

At the top of each page

  • The customer's name and phone number
  • Where they live or work, so you can find them
  • The credit limit you agreed, so you know when to stop giving

The 5 columns

  1. Date: the day of the sale or payment.
  2. Details: what they took (“rice 5 kg, oil 1 L”) or how they paid (“cash”, “transfer”).
  3. Credit: the value of goods they took and will pay for later.
  4. Paid: money they gave you.
  5. Balance: what they owe after this line.
New balance = previous balance + credit − paid

Work it out on every line, not at the end of the month. A mistake then shows up on the next line, not weeks later. For big amounts, ask the customer to initial the line.

Amounts are in your own money. Names are made up.

If you make a mistake

Don't erase it or tear out the page. Draw one line through the wrong entry, write the right one on a new line and initial it. You and the customer can both see what changed, and nobody argues later.

The summary page (once a month)

At the front of the book, list every customer on the last day of the month.

Two numbers matter most:

  • Total owed is your money that is not in your shop. Many owners are surprised how big it is.
  • Days since last paid shows who has gone quiet. Remind them first, politely and in private. Need the words? Polite WhatsApp reminder messages.

Questions

What columns should a shop credit book have?

Five: Date, Details, Credit (what the customer took), Paid (what they gave you) and Balance (what they owe after that line). Put the customer's name, phone and credit limit at the top of their page.

How do I work out a customer's balance?

New balance = previous balance + credit - paid. Work it out on every line so a mistake shows up on the next line, not weeks later.

What if I make a mistake in the book?

Don't erase or tear out the page. Draw one line through the wrong entry, write the correct one on a new line and initial it, so you and the customer can both see what changed.
